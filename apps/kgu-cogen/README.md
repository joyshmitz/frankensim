# Gas-Piston Cogeneration Unit (`apps/kgu-cogen`)

A physics engine for a 1 000 kW gas-piston cogeneration unit (CHP genset), stage 1: gas admission, engine (brake side), shaft, synchronous generator on an infinite three-phase bus, the generator-breaker cabinet (breaker, protection, power-quality analyser), genset controller and heat recovery. It is a description on the FrankenSim composition kernel (`fs-ports` Component contract **v0**, `fs-system`), built natively and to WebAssembly. There is no page here: the engine is the package a teaching control console loads.

The machine is the training station's conventional unit (1 000 kW electric, 1 500 rpm, 12 cylinders, 0.4 kV generator, 0.4/10 kV unit transformer). Its passport numbers are themselves invented and plausible, as their source says; they belong to no real machine.

The plant advances in fixed 10 ms sub-steps, the governor's sample period. The swing of the generator against the bus is about 4.6 Hz with the assumed inertia and reactances, so one period takes 21 sub-steps. `step(dt)` takes whole sub-steps and carries the remainder; a run is the same at any calling rate, and two runs of the same commands agree to the bit. Measured on the built package under Node 25: 2.0 ms of wall time per simulated second (20 µs per sub-step), so a console has some 500 times real time in hand.

## Quick start

Builds go through `rch` from the repository root (the owner's rule for this host); the engine has its own `[workspace]`.

```bash
# native tests
RCH_REQUIRE_REMOTE=1 rch exec -- cargo test --locked --manifest-path apps/kgu-cogen/engine-kgu/Cargo.toml -j 8

# WebAssembly package (rch syncs the artifact into target/ at the repository root)
RCH_REQUIRE_REMOTE=1 RCH_BUILD_TIMEOUT_SEC=1500 rch exec -- cargo build --locked \
  --manifest-path apps/kgu-cogen/engine-kgu/Cargo.toml -j 8 --target wasm32-unknown-unknown --release
cd apps/kgu-cogen && npm run wasm:bindgen   # wasm-bindgen 0.2.128, --target web, into wasm-pkg/
npm test                                    # Node tests of the package
```

## The plant

```text
gas supply -> gas valve ==fuel stream==> engine --+
                                                  |  one shaft speed
                 starter (battery) ---------------+-- shaft inertia
                 generator (stiff bus, three phase voltages) ---+
controller --signals--> starter, gas valve, ignition, close request and reset to the cabinet
cabinet (protection) --signal--> generator breaker      analyser, meter: read the bus, switch nothing

engine jacket, exhaust exchanger, plate exchanger, dump cooler -- one coolant temperature
plate exchanger -> district heating     dump cooler -> ambient air     exhaust exchanger -> stack
```

What is a state and therefore emerges: shaft speed and angle, the generator's load angle (also with the breaker open: it is the phase the synchroniser watches), the coolant temperature, the gas valve's lift, the governor's integral, the controller's sequence step, latches and timers, the gas meter's index, the cabinet's timers, latches and the rotor's thermal replica.

### What is fitted, and what the tests then check

Fitted to the passport: the electrical efficiency against load (exactly, at 1.00 pu excitation), and the thermal recovery at rated load (through one tube length). Checked, with tolerances stated in the tests: electrical efficiency at 100 % and 50 % within 0.3 points of passport × 0.955 (the plant gives it to four digits); thermal recovery at 100 % within 1.5 points (42.75 % against 43.0 %).

Not fitted, and compared with the passport all the same: thermal recovery is 45.2 % against 45.0 % at 75 % load, 48.7 % against 47.0 % at 50 %, and **53.7 % against 49.0 % at 30 %**. The low-load overshoot is the model's defect: a constant wall-heat share and a friction power that does not fall with load put too much heat into the jacket there.

Away from 1.00 pu excitation the electrical efficiency moves with the generator's losses (more stator current, more core flux); that is computed.

## Not Modelled

- The in-cylinder cycle: no pressure trace, no combustion, no air path, turbocharger, mixture or knock. Brake behaviour is the fitted card. Cylinders are a number. Per-cylinder exhaust temperatures and misfire are absent.
- Mass balance. Air and exhaust are not streams of the description, so the kernel's mass account is not claimed (`mass_residual_kg()` is `None`); only the fuel's mass is counted, at the supply boundary. The energy account is complete.
- Entropy of the fuel stream and of combustion (contract v0 carries no stream entropy). Components book the entropy of the heat they release.
- The oil circuit. Low oil pressure is an input that trips after its delay; pre-lubrication is a timer.
- The standby heater that keeps the block at 60 °C: only the starting temperature.
- Thermal masses of the exchangers, the coolant's transport delay round the circuit, the heating network's own inertia. With them missing, a failed cooler trips the unit in seconds to a minute and a half where a real plant takes minutes.
- Stator and field transients, the voltage regulator, saturation, sub-transient currents, the transformer's losses and tap changer. Excitation is the EMF directly. The breaker closes and opens at once. A manual close at the edge of the window gives the jolt the equations give; no closing-time compensation.
- Where the three voltages are. They are the voltages of the stiff bus, in per unit of 230.9 V phase to neutral. The unit transformer is a reactance lumped into the machine and an ideal ratio: its winding connection, which on a real plant changes how a single-phase fault upstream looks on the 0.4 kV side, is absent, and the cabinet measures the bus, not a terminal voltage that differs from it by the drop over the transformer.
- Zero sequence: no neutral current, no earth fault, no short circuit of any kind. The three inputs are voltages, never faults; the over-current stages answer only to what the machine equations give (a weak field, an overload).
- A bus without voltage is a dead bus, not a short circuit. With all three phases at exactly zero the generator behind closed contacts carries no current and no power (the supply is gone and nothing loads the bus), and the cabinet's under-voltage function opens the breaker after its delay. A stiff bus at a small voltage, on the other hand, draws what the machine equations give, which is large. Between the two the model has a step that a real network does not have: fault currents, their decay, and an island with load are not modelled.
- The 100 Hz torque ripple and vibration that go with a negative-sequence current; the rotor's temperature itself; the change of `R2` and `X2` with frequency and saturation. The negative-sequence function is the relay's replica, not a thermal model of the rotor: above its continuous level (8 %) it counts `(I2/In)² t` against the limit without cooling, below it it only cools. A current just above the level therefore trips in the end (after 52 min at 8.01 %) and one just below never does. That step is the relay characteristic's, and a heat balance of the rotor would not have it.
- `STATOR_CURRENT_A` is the current of the positive sequence. On an unbalanced bus a phase carries more; `LARGEST_PHASE_CURRENT_A` and the three phase currents show it, and the over-current stages watch the phases.
- Protection functions not in the cabinet: vector shift, rate of change of frequency, differential, earth fault, voltage-dependent over-current, loss of excitation as a function of its own (a weak field shows up as over-current). Definite times only, no inverse curves. No breaker-failure logic; the breaker opens in the sub-step of the trip.
- Which cabinet functions stop the engine: over-current (both stages) and reverse power are treated as faults of the unit and stop it; voltage, unbalance, negative sequence and frequency are grid events and leave it running unloaded. A real over-current can also be a through-fault of the grid; that distinction needs fault modelling, which is absent.
- In the analyser: harmonics, flicker, transients shorter than the 10 ms sub-step, RMS over the standard's half-cycle windows and its averaging intervals (10 min and the like), frequency and unbalance statistics. Harmonics and the standard's weekly assessment, in both directions (what the unit delivers and what it receives), are planned as separate models; the engine solves the fundamental only. An event's start and duration are whole sub-steps. Its measurements of current, power and frequency are the snapshot's own; it adds only the event record and its clock.
- In the meter: its accuracy class and any error of measurement (its energy is the engine's own, and its voltage at a look is the bus voltage exactly); current and voltage transformers; tariff zones, load profiles, demand maxima; the minimum, maximum and exact duration of a voltage event, which such a meter does not know; hysteresis on its levels (none); apparent energy. The closing instant of a record is kept, as a look of the meter's clock; a meter that keeps only the opening instant would show less. What a full journal does (displace the oldest record) is an assumption of the card. The meter reads the bus side of the breaker, like the analyser. Both instruments stamp with their own clocks as those stand at the moment: setting a clock back while a record is open gives a closing time before the opening time, and nothing in the record says the clock was set.
- Breakaway and stick-slip of the shaft. Rest is entered and kept by rule: the starter not engaged, the driving torque of the last accepted sub-step no more than the static friction, the speed below what static friction stops in a sub-step. The comparison is with the torque of the sub-step before, not of the sub-step itself, so a torque that appears from nowhere moves a resting shaft one sub-step late; the starter, which is the only thing that does that here, is known by its command and is not late. No turning gear, no barring, no reverse rotation at the stop. The kernel's own tier for dry friction (a component stepped on the load line its neighbours present) is not used: it needs the friction as a component of its own beside neighbours that declare their tangents, which the engine, starter and generator here do not.
- Gas train beyond one valve: no regulator, no filter, no leak test. Compressibility is one.
- Events are taken at sub-step boundaries (10 ms); the kernel's event location is not used.
- Acoustics, vibration, fields, the design optimiser: later slices.

## Kernel limits met

- A component inside a looped block must be continuous and have a slope on both sides wherever the solve can land. A step function, or a corner with a flat side, stalls the kernel's Newton search (`did not converge`), because its backtracking cannot step across. Found three times here and removed in the app: an exchanger that switched on with the flow, an engine law that branched on "fuel flows", a piecewise-linear thermostat. The laws are now smooth where the solve iterates and branch only on signals held over a step.
- The controller is therefore outside the loop: it reads measurements sampled from the accepted state after each sub-step (start-of-step feedback) and drives the plant through signals. Wiring its inputs as kernel signals from the plant would put a state machine inside the shaft's Newton block.
- The cabinet's protection is outside the loop for the same reason: thresholds and latches. It reads the bus voltages and frequency as set, and the currents and power of the last accepted sub-step, and reaches the breaker by a forward signal in the sub-step in which a timer runs out. The controller learns of the trip one sub-step (10 ms) later.
- A differenced input must not be a subnormal number. For a component without a declared tangent the kernel differences its port map with a step of `sqrt(eps)` times the input's size, and falls back to a step of one unit only when that size is exactly zero (`fs-system/src/system.rs`, `tangent_column`). An input below about 1.7e-316 gives a step that rounds to zero, the difference quotient is 0/0, and the block is refused as having a singular interface Jacobian, at that state for ever. (Read from the kernel's code, not instrumented; the size it predicts, 1.66e-316 rad/s, is the speed at which the refusal was measured.) Until slice 1e this plant got there by itself: with the regularised friction the speed of a stopped unit decayed by 9 % per sub-step and reached that size 86 s after an emergency stop, in the speed input of the engine, the starter and the generator; a unit that had never turned stood at exact zero and was not affected. The shaft now comes to rest at exact zero (above). The kernel's step has no floor still.
- A refused step leaves the system at its last accepted state (the kernel's step is atomic). `step()` then returns false, drops the time it still owed, and the next call starts from that state; commands given before the refused step are still waiting in their queue. Earlier editions of this file said that no setter of the package could provoke a refusal. That was wrong twice: a gas pressure of zero with the ignition on let the shaft's speed decay into subnormal numbers again (the rest rule looked at the ignition's command), and a network return temperature above the coolant's met a corner in the plate exchanger's law. Both are removed, and a random walk over every setter and command, with values from inside and outside their ranges (four seeds natively, eight on the package, thousands of actions and hours of model time each), now checks that `step()` never refuses, that no number stops being one and that the ledger closes. It is a search, not a proof: it found the second of the two.
- No rotor circuit in the `fs-phs` synchronous machine (stator and field only), so nothing in the kernel computes negative-sequence rotor heating; no protection relay or sequence-component element in `fs-control`.
- No synchronous-machine Component in `fs-phs` behind `fs-ports`, no droop, sequencer or synchroniser in `fs-control`, no signal-modulated conductance among the kernel's elements: those are the in-app models above.

## The WebAssembly boundary

`KguSim` (see `wasm-pkg/fs_kgu_cogen.d.ts`): `new()`; setters `set_load_setpoint_pct`, `set_excitation_pu`, `set_heat_demand_frac`, `set_network_return_temp_c`, `set_gas_gauge_kpa`, `set_gas_temp_c`, `set_grid_voltage_pu`, `set_grid_freq_hz`, `set_fan_failed`, `set_oil_pressure_low`, and beyond the first contract `set_auto_sync`, `set_atm_pressure_kpa`, `set_condition_factor`, `set_load_ramp_pct_per_min`; commands `cmd_start`, `cmd_stop`, `cmd_emergency_stop`, `cmd_ack`, `cmd_breaker_close` (returns 0 when accepted, else refusal bits), `cmd_breaker_open`; `step(dt_s)`; `snapshot()`, `limits()`, `events()`, `machine_facts()`, `last_error()`.

For the three-phase bus and the cabinet: `set_grid_phase_voltages_pu(a, b, c)` (each 0 to 1.3 of the rated phase voltage; `set_grid_voltage_pu(x)` sets all three to `x`), `set_grid_phase_angles_deg(a, b, c)` (offsets from the balanced positions), `set_restoration_mode(0 | 1)` (manual, the default, or automatic), `set_analyser_clock_offset_s(s)`, `analyser_events()` (five numbers per closed event: kind, phase, start by the analyser's clock, duration, residual or highest voltage in volts) and `clear_analyser_events()`. A cabinet trip is an event of kind 7 in `events()`, in model time: its code is ten times the function's index plus the phase (1 to 3, or 0), its value what it tripped at. `cmd_ack()` also resets the cabinet's latch. The 19 snapshot slots and 34 limits added for this are appended after the earlier ones, whose order is unchanged; the layout version stays 1.

For the meter: `set_meter_clock_offset_s(s)` (how far its clock is ahead of model time), `set_meter_sample_phase_s(s)` (where within its period it looks at the voltages; taken modulo the period), `meter_events()` (four numbers per record: journal 1 low voltage, 2 high voltage, 3 loss of voltage; phases seen as bits; the look that saw the condition and the look that saw it gone, NaN until then, both by the meter's clock; the low-voltage journal first, then high, then loss, oldest first within each) and `clear_meter_events()` (journals and overflow flags, not the registers). For synchronisation on command: `cmd_synchronise()` (returns 0 when accepted, else the refusal bits of `cmd_breaker_close`: 16 not synchronising, 32 a trip is latched, and since slice 1f 8 dead bus and 64 bus not fit); it is an event of kind 8 in `events()` whose code is that return value and whose value is the phase angle at the command. Ten more snapshot slots (four registers, the meter's clock, the three journals' record counts, the overflow bits, `SYNCHRONISER_ARMED_BY_OPERATOR`) and eight more limits (the meter's card) are appended in the same way; then two more limits, `SHAFT_STATIC_FRICTION_NM` and `SHAFT_REST_SPEED_RPM`. At rest `SPEED_RPM` is exactly 0.

Commands. Each `cmd_*` call puts its command in a queue; the controller takes one per sub-step, in the order given (an emergency stop first), and every command ends in the event log as an event of kind 9: its code the command (1 start, 2 stop, 3 emergency stop, 4 acknowledge, 5 breaker close, 6 breaker open, 7 synchronise), its value 0 when it was carried out, else the reasons it was refused. So `cmd_ack(); cmd_start()` with no step between is an acknowledgement and then a start, 10 ms apart, and a command that has no meaning where it arrives (a start while running, an acknowledgement with nothing latched) is refused in the log, not dropped. A stop that arrives in the sub-step of a protection trip is carried out. Refusal bits, added to those of `cmd_breaker_close`: 64 the bus is not fit by the cabinet's settings, 128 not applicable in this step of the sequence, 256 the cause of the trip is still present, 512 too many commands waiting (32). `cmd_breaker_close()` and `cmd_synchronise()` still answer at once from the present state, and the controller takes the permission again in the sub-step in which it acts.

`step(dt)` takes `dt` whole or not at all: a step longer than `longest_step_s()` (3 600 s), or not a positive number, returns false with the reason in `last_error()` and advances nothing. The same time asked for in one call or in parts gives the same state to the bit.

Added in slice 1f, appended like the others: alarm bits 18 (synchronising time limit) and 19 (engine stall); event kinds 9 (command outcome) and 10 (synchronising time limit: code what kept the breaker out, value the seconds it stood there); snapshot slots `LARGEST_PHASE_CURRENT_A`, `ANALYSER_DISPLACED_EVENTS`, `SYNCHRONISING_TIME_LEFT_S`, `RETURN_TIME_LEFT_S`, `PENDING_COMMANDS`, `BUS_FIT_TO_CLOSE`; limits `SYNCHRONISING_TIME_LIMIT_S`, `ANALYSER_RECORD_CAPACITY`. Changed in meaning, not in place: `BREAKER_CLOSED` and the breaker events follow the cabinet's contacts; `REACTIVE_POWER_KVAR` and the meter's reactive registers include the negative sequence; `COOLANT_RETURN_C` is the supply less what the network exchanger and the dump cooler take (it was the supply less what the engine puts in, which is the same only in a steady state: 68.3 °C where it was 68.2 °C at full load); a protection trip of several functions is several events of kind 7; a deep voltage excursion is a dip and an interruption in the analyser, and one low-voltage and one loss record in the meter; the grid event's alarm stays latched through an automatic return until the breaker is in.

`snapshot()` is a flat `Float64Array`. Its layout is versioned in slot 0 and named by the engine (`snapshot_field_names()`); `snapshotLayout.mjs` mirrors it with index constants, state codes, alarm bits, refusal bits, event kinds, protection functions, the meter's journals and decoders for the three records, and a test fails when the two disagree. A slot with nothing to show holds NaN.

The engine returns numbers and field names only. The station controller's event codes (A-105 high coolant temperature, A-150 low gas pressure, A-160 low oil pressure, A-031 mains under-voltage, W-210 failed start, W-118 cooler fan) are bit positions in `ALARM_BITS` and appear as text in comments and in this file, never in a string the package returns; a Node test checks the package for them.

Protection as modelled: A-105 trips when the coolant state reaches 98 °C; A-150 when the gas valve is fully open, the power falls short of the load reference and the supply is below the pressure switch, for 5 s; A-160 is input-driven with its delay; A-031 is the cabinet's phase under-voltage (any phase below 0.85 pu for 0.15 s), which since slice 1c opens the breaker and no longer stops the engine; over-speed at 115 %; out-of-step on a pole slip; W-210 when a start does not reach rated speed in 30 s of cranking and run-up (the pre-lubrication before them is not counted); a stall when the ignition is on and the speed is below the firing speed of 120 rpm (the gas has gone: about 11 s after it goes at no load); the synchronising time limit when the unit has stood at the synchroniser for 180 s, which stops it normally and latches until acknowledged.

## Tests

Native (`engine-kgu/src/tests.rs`, `cards.rs`, `analyser.rs`, `meter.rs`): 82 tests. Four of them, in `cards.rs` and named `card_check_…`, compare a card with itself (a map inverts, a fit returns the point it was fitted to): they guard the cards' arithmetic and are no evidence about the plant. The test that the meter's active registers equal the engine's counter is a check of the transfer, both being the kernel ledger's energy, and says so. The cabinet's functions are tested against their own card: that the device does what it is set to; the settings themselves are inputs to be verified. Every in-app Component passes `fs_ports::component::conformance::check_component` at several operating points (purity, the first-law audit, admission, cancellation). Scenario tests each check a quantity that was not an input: steady 100 % and 50 % load; droop; full-load rejection; synchronisation refused outside the window and accepted inside; excitation against reactive power; start from standstill; a start without gas; normal stop (cool-down burns gas, coast-down burns none, to the bit); emergency stop and acknowledgement; low gas pressure; oil pressure and under-voltage; heat to the network; heat to the dump cooler; fan failure to the coolant trip; partial demand; bit-for-bit repeatability. The ledger's relative residual stays below 1e-9 in every scenario that checks it (measured: 1e-14).

For the three-phase bus and the cabinet: sequence components; the generator's negative-sequence current and where its heat comes from; every protection function at its setting and the `1 / I2²` law of the rotor's replica; a balanced bus against the numbers recorded before the bus had three phases; one phase at 0.73 pu; all three at 0.73 pu; unbalance without low voltage; a small unbalance that runs on; automatic restoration (return, lasting disturbance, flickering grid); manual restoration; over-current as a unit fault; the analyser's record and clock.

For the meter and the commanded synchronisation: a 130 ms dip between two looks (analyser one event, meter none, breaker closed) and with a look inside it (a record without depth or duration); the share of a hundred sample phases that catch it (13); a 3 s dip in both records by two clocks; the 101st event against the journal's capacity, and both overflow behaviours at the instrument; the four registers against the engine's counter and the sign of the reactive power; manual return with no command, with the command, with the command before the acknowledgement, with a stop after the command; a start without automatic synchronisation, with the command and with a manual closing.

For standstill: the shaft component's conformance and its law (a slow undriven shaft ends the step at exactly zero and releases its kinetic energy and the step's work as heat; a resting one stays; a driven one is the kernel's inertia); a normal stop, two hours at rest checked every minute for exactly zero speed, a start and load; the same after an emergency stop and acknowledgement; starts interrupted while pre-lubricating and while cranking, and a start that fails without gas, each followed by ten minutes at rest and in the end a start; a refused step (native only) that leaves the last accepted state and is followed by an accepted one.

From the third round of review, each a test that failed before its fix: an engine with ignition and no gas stalls, is tripped with its own alarm and stands (waiting for the grid, cooling down, at the synchroniser); all three phases gone trips the cabinet's under-voltage after its delay, with its event; no closing onto a bus with a phase below the cabinet's setting, by request or by the armed synchroniser; a synchronisation that cannot be made stopped at its time limit with the reason; a stop on each sub-step around a protection trip; commands given without a step between, in order, each with its outcome; a breaker opening on each sub-step around a trip; a step longer than an hour refused whole, and an hour in one call against an hour in parts, to the bit; the lock-out and the longest wait against a switch of the mode; a grid that turns bad during the return; two functions tripping in one sub-step; the negative sequence in the reactive power; the return line after an emergency stop; the analyser's displaced events and its separate interruption; the meter's one record of a deep disturbance; a breaker opening while unloading; the jolt of a manual closing ahead of and behind the bus; and the random walk (four seeds, 3 000 actions and more than an hour of model time each; each walk must have started the unit, run it on the bus and had the cabinet trip it, or it does not count).

Node (`test/kguEngine.test.mjs`): 47 tests, the same scenarios against the built package, plus the layout check and the check that no event code is in the package. The balanced-bus test holds the package to the recorded numbers to 1e-12 (they are reproduced digit for digit); natively the same numbers are met to 1e-7: the native build differs from the package in the ninth digit (1000.000003 kW against 1000.000001 kW), which was not investigated and is presumed to come from the two targets' elementary functions.
